## Overall assessment

The notebook contains the substantive work for every major milestone component, including the later experiments, XAI, and inference sections. They are not placeholders. Its strongest parts are the explicit key/grain checks, chronological split, shifted historical features, training-fitted preprocessing, common model inputs, validation-only model/threshold selection code, controlled experiments, and SHAP prediction-reconstruction checks.

## Findings affecting correctness or validity

### F01 — Entry eligibility and “actually started” are not fully established

**Requirements:** R02/R03/R09/R12: entrant-level predictions, pre-race availability, and mechanical failures per race start. **Locations:** §§1.1.6, 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 3.2; cells 26, 115–116, 138, 155–157, 178–179.

**Confirmed evidence:** cell 179 defines `started` by excluding `positionText` F/W and three status strings. It removes 64 entries and then calls the remaining 8,906 rows starters. This is a cohort change relative to all 8,970 entries. Q1 simply uses positive grid positions 1/2; Q2 additionally requires `grid > 0`; Q3 removes F/W/Withdrew and treats remaining rows, including grid-zero rows, as starts. These are different operational populations, only partly explained by each question's purpose.

**Risk needing verification:** a recorded mechanical reason or a grid assignment does not by itself demonstrate that the driver took the race start. Conversely, zero completed laps can describe a genuine first-lap retirement, so blindly excluding `laps == 0` would not resolve this. The saved notebook has no independent start-status reconciliation or time-stamped evidence that every excluded withdrawal was already known at the intended prediction instant. Using outcome-derived eligibility can create selection bias if the removal could not have been made at that instant.

A read-only CSV spot-check applying cell 179's inclusion rule reproduced the 8,906 retained rows and found 244 with zero completed laps. One retained row has both grid zero and zero laps: race 983 (2017 Malaysian GP), driver 8, status `Battery`, position text `R`. This is a concrete case requiring start-status verification; the CSV fields alone do not establish whether it was a pit-lane start or a failure before the start. No external incident account was used to resolve it.

**Practical impact:** the model estimates performance conditional on its inclusion rule; it does not demonstrate identical performance for every entered driver. Mechanical-rate denominators and front-row conversion denominators may also differ from literal race starts. This is a confirmed definition/verification gap, not proof that all 64 exclusions are wrong or that a particular score is inflated.

### F02 — Mechanical status categories are proxies for cause, not verified component failures

**Requirements:** R12/R13/R15/R33: the explicit mechanical-retirement definition, justified features, and supported conclusions. **Locations:** §§2.3.1–2.3.6, 3.4.1, 3.4.4; cells 155–168, 190–191, 202.

**Confirmed evidence:** cell 155 includes `Front wing`, `Rear wing`, `Undertray`, `Seat` and `Vibrations` as mechanical, while excluding `Wheel`, `Wheel nut`, `Tyre` and `Puncture` on assumed team-error/contact grounds. There is no event-level evidence distinguishing spontaneous failures from collision damage or other causes. Cell 163 varies only `Retired`. Its description as “the upper bound” in cell 162 therefore means an upper scenario for that one ambiguous category, not an upper bound on all true mechanical retirements.

The historical extension in cell 190 leaves ten cases uncounted, including `Driver Seat`, `Fire` and `Handling`; the contemporary mapping includes `Seat`. Cell 191 says ten rows “cannot move the feature,” but a changed event can affect multiple subsequent rolling windows even if its aggregate effect is small.

**Practical impact:** saved answers such as Lotus F1 18.7% and Alfa Romeo 12.5% are reproducible status-rule rates, but their strict interpretation as component-failure rates is not fully verified. The same taxonomy affects `team_mech_dnf_rate`. Actual misclassifications and their size require event review or sensitivity analysis; this audit does not assume every ambiguous category is incorrect.

### F03 — The Spearman p-value does not test whether circuit rankings changed

**Requirements:** R10/R33/R37: answer the era-ranking question with defensible evidence. **Locations:** §2.1.3–2.1.4; cells 125–126, 132.

**Confirmed evidence:** cell 125 runs `spearmanr` on the two era-rate vectors and prints rho 0.30, p 0.29. Cell 126 uses this p-value to discuss whether the ranking's change is “real” or random. The tested null is zero rank association between the two vectors, not equality of rankings or equality of each circuit's conversion rates across eras. Failure to reject zero association cannot quantify how much observed reshuffling is sampling noise.

**Practical impact:** the displayed descriptive rank changes remain valid summaries of the saved counts. The p-value does not substantiate their statistical significance, or the fraction attributable to noise. Small samples are appropriately acknowledged, but uncertainty about the changes is not actually estimated.

### F04 — “No home advantage” is stronger than the fitted model establishes

**Requirements:** R11/R33/R37: starting-position-controlled comparison and justified conclusions. **Locations:** §2.2.4–2.2.5; cells 143–149, and feature discussion in cell 212.

**Confirmed evidence:** the saved adjusted odds ratio is 0.954 with 95% CI 0.808–1.126; the modern-era check is 0.778 with CI 0.523–1.158. Both permit effects above and below one. Cell 149 opens with a categorical “No”; cell 147 says a real effect would need p < 0.05. Nonsignificance establishes insufficient evidence under this model, not absence of an effect, and existence of an effect does not depend on a significance threshold. Odds ratios compare odds, not probability ratios, despite the loose “chance” description.

Grid groups do meet the requirement to compare similar starting positions, and race-clustered uncertainty is a strength. However, the model controls only broad bands and fits a common home coefficient; it does not prove that all within-band grid differences or driver/era differences have been removed. Cell 212's univariate home-feature AUC is not an additional adjusted test.

**Practical impact:** the defensible conclusion is no clear evidence of a home advantage after the chosen adjustment. Feature rejection remains a documented heuristic, but cannot be justified as proof that home status has no conditional predictive value.

### F06 — The feature-effect plot splits tied values using row order

**Requirements:** R13/R33: valid exploratory evidence supporting feature choices. **Locations:** §3.5.2, cells 214–215.

**Confirmed implementation issue:** `podium_rate_by_bins` claims “ties merged,” but uses `pd.qcut(df[feature].rank(method="first"), q=10, ...)`. Ranking with `method="first"` gives equal-valued observations different ranks according to row order. It therefore forces ties into separate deciles instead of merging them. The saved form-rate plots visibly contain several points with the same zero-valued bin median.

The modeling table is ordered by race and driver, so tied observations can be divided partly by time/driver ordering. Different podium rates at identical feature values are then plotted as if they were a feature-value progression. The statement that seven features have monotonic relationships is also too absolute for plots with small reversals and repeated x coordinates.

**Practical impact:** the implementation can distort the visual evidence about functional shape, especially zero-heavy podium rates. It does not change the feature values supplied to the models; the separate AUC/correlation/ablation evidence still exists.

### F07 — Qualifying-gap and historical-form labels do not exactly describe the calculations

**Requirements:** R12/R13/R15/R31/R37: documented feature meanings and consistent inference. **Locations:** §§3.4.1–3.4.4, 3.5.6 and 7.1; cells 188–202, 227, 285.

**Confirmed evidence:** `quali_gap_pct` is built from each driver's minimum of Q1/Q2/Q3, divided by the minimum of those values across the race. Cells 188, 197, 215, 233 and the inference validation message call this a gap to pole. The function never selects qualifying position 1 or the decisive pole-setting session, so equivalence to the pole time is not guaranteed. Cross-session conditions and progression can affect the number. The training comparison in cell 198 supports predictive utility, not the stronger physical “real pace” explanation in cell 199.

Cell 202 also computes form/reliability over **all cleaned result entries**, without applying the starter filter from cell 179. `career_starts` is `cumcount()` over those entries. Thus failed-to-qualify/withdrawn entries can enter history, and the history windows count previous entries for drivers, while team rates average entry fractions per team–race. The notebook partly acknowledges the career count as entries, but elsewhere calls these previous races/starts and mechanical-retirement rates as though the populations matched §2.3.

**Practical impact:** these remain past-only predictors, so this is not direct target leakage. Their interpretation and upstream construction for inference can nonetheless be wrong if a caller implements literal pole-time gaps or starter-only histories. The reliability feature is not exactly the same operational rate used in the relational answer.

### F08 — Removing grid position loses information when qualifying is missing; relevance claims are too categorical

**Requirements:** R13/R15/R33: justify the selected feature set and handle missingness. **Locations:** §§3.4.4–3.6; cells 202, 205–206, 211–224, 234–236.

**Confirmed evidence:** cell 224 states that grid information is not lost because `quali_position + grid_penalty` equals grid position. That identity holds only when qualifying position is known. In cell 202, missing qualifying makes both selected columns missing even when the original grid is known. After `grid_position` is dropped, median filling cannot reconstruct that row's original grid. Cell 205 reports qualifying-position gaps in both training and validation.

Cell 220 also equates correlations around 0.9 with “no new information” and says permutation/SHAP importance would each show half the effect. Neither conclusion follows from rank correlation; credit depends on the fitted model and explanation method. The later, more careful XAI discussion in cell 282 does not erase this earlier claim. AUC near 0.5 for a rare binary feature also does not by itself prove “no signal”; it measures that feature's marginal ranking utility in this population.

**Practical impact:** the saved selection rule is transparent but heuristic, not proof that all discarded information is redundant. Missing-qualifying rows specifically lose an otherwise available pre-race predictor. The effect on scores has not been isolated by an experiment.

### F09 — “Unseen test” and “training-only choices” require a narrower interpretation

**Requirements:** R03/R14/R21/R33: chronological integrity and selection without test results. **Locations:** §§1–3, 4.7 and 5.4; cells 115–168, 170, 174–175, 183, 188, 236–239, 254–255, 270.

**Confirmed evidence:** before §4's test evaluation, §2 studies podium/mechanical outcomes through 2024 and §3.1 scores individual fields against all 2003–2024 podium labels. Cell 188 explicitly uses §2's findings to motivate candidates. Test feature means and missingness are also displayed in §3.6. Consequently, cell 170's claim that test seasons never influence the feature set, and cell 183's “touched once,” are broader than the visible workflow supports.

The actual window/gap/relevance selections in §§3.4–3.5 use training keys; preprocessing fits training only; model hyperparameters and thresholds use validation only. No confirmed test-metric-based tuning is visible. Computing historical features using earlier races within validation/test is legitimate for rolling race-by-race predictions: those outcomes are available before a later race. It would not be valid for forecasting an entire season at once without updating history, which is not what this implementation demonstrates.

**Practical impact:** call this a chronological holdout excluded from model fitting and explicit tuning, with prior exploratory exposure. It is not a fully blind test cohort. The magnitude of any exploratory selection optimism cannot be inferred from the notebook. Likewise, post-test §5 experiments are appropriately described as diagnostics and do not promote a replacement model.

### F11 — Shared-drive explanations and retained-data caveats need more precision

**Requirements:** R06/R09/R11/R33: justified duplicate handling, correct grain and analysis. **Locations:** §§1.1.4, 1.3.3, 1.4; cells 19–20, 64–67, 78–85, 105.
